Dailyvokab Privacy Policy

Last updated 23 November 2026

Dailyvokab is an app for learning Thai words. This page explains, in plain words, what the app collects and who helps us run it. It also says how long the data is kept, what your rights are, and how to switch it off or have it deleted.

On this page, “we” and “us” mean Adam Vasica, the developer who makes Dailyvokab (see “Who is responsible”). “Settings” means the Settings tab in the app.

The short version. Your learning progress is saved on your phone (and in your own iPhone backup, if you use one). While Help improve the app is on, the app also sends usage statistics (for example, which words you rate and how) and crash reports, so we can fix problems and add words. This switch is on from the moment you install the app, until you switch it off in Settings → App. When the app starts or comes back on screen, it asks RevenueCat whether Dailyvokab Pro is active. If you buy Pro, RevenueCat also checks the purchase. Each time the app starts, it also asks Expo whether a fix for the app is waiting. None of this includes your name, your email address or your exact location, and we never use it for advertising or to track you across other apps.

Your right to say no. You can object to the usage statistics and crash reports at any time. This is your right under EU and UK data protection law (GDPR Article 21). To do it, open Settings → App and turn off Help improve the app. It is free, and every part of the app still works.

Who is responsible

Dailyvokab is made by Adam Vasica, an independent developer in the Czech Republic. He decides how the data on this page is used, so he is responsible for it. In EU data protection law this is called the “controller”.

For anything about your data, write to vasica8@seznam.cz.

What is saved on your phone

Everything about your learning is saved on your phone: the words you have rated, your review schedule, your streak and learning goal, your custom units, your reminder and widget settings, whether Dailyvokab Pro is active, and the answers you gave when you set up the app. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, this data is also in your backup. There are no accounts and no server of ours that holds this data. Deleting the app deletes it from your phone; a copy can stay in an iPhone backup made before that.

While “Help improve the app” is on, the app also sends usage events about your learning, such as each word you rate and how, and your quiz results (see “Usage statistics” below).

Files you import (for example a CSV from your teacher) are read on your phone and are never uploaded. A backup you make in Settings (“Back up my progress”) is a file you save wherever you choose. It holds your progress and your custom units, not your settings. The app does not upload it.

Daily word reminders, the Smart review reminder and the Word of the hour widget are all set up on your phone by the app. They do not use any server. For the widget, the app saves the words planned for up to three weeks ahead in a place on your phone that the widget can read. Anyone who can see your phone can see these words, even when it is locked.

All the Thai and English audio comes with the app. It is an AI voice. Playing a word does not contact any server. Words you import are read by your phone's own voice, on your phone.

Usage statistics (only while “Help improve the app” is on)

To learn how the app is used, it sends usage events to PostHog, an analytics service. Our PostHog project is hosted in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany). For example:

  • a unit was opened, a Flashcards round was finished, which word you rated and how, a word was hidden, and a quiz result (mistakes and time taken)
  • how many words you know or are learning, when you make or restore a backup
  • the screens you view, the onboarding steps you reach, and settings such as your daily goal or reminder time
  • whether a search found anything; for a search that found nothing, the search text itself, cut to 40 characters and never sent if it contains a number or an “@” (it tells us which words to add)
  • when the app opens and closes, the app version, the phone model, the iOS version, and the phone's language and time zone settings
  • whether the Dailyvokab Pro screen was shown and whether a purchase was started, finished or cancelled (never a price or your Apple Account)

Each installation gets a random code so that events from the same phone can be counted together. You can see this code in the app under Settings → App → Your usage code. It is not connected to your name, email address, phone number or Apple Account. PostHog does not store your internet (IP) address for us, and the app turns off PostHog's location lookup.

Crash reports (only while “Help improve the app” is on)

If the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, it sends a technical report to Sentry, an error-tracking service. Our Sentry project stores its data in the European Union (Frankfurt). A report holds, for example, the error itself, the phone model, the iOS version, the app version, free memory, the phone's language and time zone, and the names of the last screens you opened. It also includes a random code for this install of the app and a code made from the ID that Apple gives this app on your phone. It never holds your name, email address or internet (IP) address, and never a screenshot of the app.

A short error text about a crash while the app was starting can also reach Expo, even with the switch off: see “App fixes” below.

Apart from this switch, your iPhone has its own setting: in the iPhone Settings app, Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → Share With App Developers. If it is on, Apple shares crash reports about Dailyvokab with us. Apple controls this, under its own privacy policy.

Dailyvokab Pro (subscriptions)

Apple handles the payment. We never see your card or payment details, and we do not learn your Apple Account.

To unlock Pro on your phone and to restore it later, the app uses RevenueCat, a subscription service. RevenueCat receives your App Store purchases of Dailyvokab Pro (which plan, the price and currency, and when it started, renews or ends) together with a random buyer code that the app creates. RevenueCat also receives your phone model, iOS version, language, App Store country, the app version and an ID that Apple gives this app on your phone. When the app contacts RevenueCat, RevenueCat also receives your internet (IP) address, as every connection does. It works out your country from it, keeps the country and drops the address. RevenueCat is based in the United States, so this information may be handled there.

RevenueCat also gives us totals, such as how many people buy each plan. We may test two different offers of Pro. If we do, RevenueCat picks at random which of the two offers you see.

Because Pro cannot work without it, the app contacts RevenueCat when it starts or comes back on screen, even when “Help improve the app” is off.

App fixes (each time the app starts)

Sometimes we fix a mistake without a new App Store version. Each time the app starts while you are online, it asks Expo whether a fix is waiting. Expo's EAS Update service is run by 650 Industries, Inc. in the United States. A fix downloads in the background and is used the next time the app starts.

With each check, Expo stores your internet (IP) address and a random code for this install of the app. It also receives the app version and the iOS version. If the app crashed while it was starting, the next check also sends a short error text about that crash (at most 1,024 characters), so a broken fix can be found and stopped fast. A check sends no learning progress and no name or email address.

Because fixes cannot reach your phone without it, this check runs even when “Help improve the app” is off.

If you email us

“Contact us” and “Report a problem with this word” open your own email app. If you send the email, we receive your email address and what you wrote. “Report a problem with this word” also fills in the word and the app version for you. We use this only to answer you and to fix the app.

Why we may use this data

EU data protection law (the GDPR; the UK has its own version of it) says we need a legal reason for each use of personal data. Personal data includes information that is linked only to a random code. These are our reasons:

  • Usage statistics and crash reports: to fix problems and to improve the app, for example to choose which words to add next. Legal reason: our legitimate interest in an app that works and gets better (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). A “legitimate interest” is a real and fair reason of our own, which must not be more important than your rights. You can object at any time by switching off “Help improve the app”.
  • Purchases: to give you the Pro features you paid for, and to restore them. Legal reason: to carry out our agreement with you (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Pro checks, totals and offer tests: to check whether Pro is active (also if you have not bought it), to show the right plans, and to see which plans and offers work best. Legal reason: our legitimate interest in offering and improving Dailyvokab Pro (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • App fix checks: to bring you fixes quickly, and to find and stop a fix that breaks the app. Legal reason: our legitimate interest in a safe app that works (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Emails: to answer you. Legal reason: our legitimate interest in answering you (Article 6(1)(f)). When you ask about your data, we answer because the law says we must (Article 6(1)(c)).

We do not sell any data, we do not share it for advertising, and we do not try to find out who you are.

Who handles the data for us

  • PostHog (usage statistics), EU hosting
  • Sentry (crash reports), EU data region, Frankfurt
  • RevenueCat (Dailyvokab Pro purchases), United States
  • Expo (app fixes: EAS Update by 650 Industries, Inc.), United States

Each of them handles the data for us, under data protection terms in its agreement with us. It may use the data only to provide its service to us, and it must protect the data at least as well as this policy says. One exception: some of them, for example Expo and RevenueCat, may also use the data in a form that no longer identifies you, to improve their own services.

These companies also receive some data, under their own privacy policies:

  • Apple: the payment and the App Store itself, your iPhone backups if you use iCloud, and crash reports if you allow them on your iPhone
  • Google: our contact address is a Gmail address, so Google stores the emails you send us
  • GitHub: it runs this website. When you open these pages, GitHub records your internet (IP) address for security.

Data outside the European Union

We are based in the Czech Republic, in the European Union. Some of the companies above are from the United States:

  • PostHog and Sentry are US companies. They store our data on servers in Frankfurt, Germany, in the EU. Their staff outside the EU, for example in the US, may still access it to run and support the service. Their data processing terms protect this with the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework or with standard contractual clauses.
  • RevenueCat handles the purchase data in the United States. Its data processing terms, which are part of its agreement with us, use the EU's standard contractual clauses. These are contract rules written by the European Commission to protect data that leaves the EU.
  • Expo handles the app fix checks in the United States. Expo says in its privacy policy that it follows the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (and its UK and Swiss versions), and that it uses standard contractual clauses where the law asks for them. The European Commission has decided that US companies in this framework protect personal data well enough.
  • Apple, Google and GitHub may also handle data outside the EU, under their own privacy policies.

To get a copy of these safeguards, write to vasica8@seznam.cz.

How long it is kept

  • Usage statistics: up to one year, then PostHog deletes them.
  • Crash reports: up to 90 days, then Sentry deletes them.
  • Purchase records: while Dailyvokab uses RevenueCat, so that Pro can be restored.
  • App fix checks: Expo deletes a start-up error text after 3 days. For the other details of a check, Expo gives no fixed time: its privacy policy says it keeps data only as long as it reasonably needs it.
  • Emails: as long as needed to answer you. Ask us and we delete them.

Switching it off

“Help improve the app” is on from the moment you install the app, until you switch it off in Settings → App.

To switch it off, open the app, go to Settings → App, and turn off Help improve the app. From that moment the app sends nothing to PostHog or Sentry, and events still waiting on the phone are discarded. Two services are still contacted, because the app needs them to work: RevenueCat, to buy and restore Dailyvokab Pro, and Expo, to check for app fixes each time the app starts. After a crash while the app was starting, that check can include a short error text, as described above.

Having your data deleted

Open Settings → App → Your usage code, tap it to copy the code, and email the code to us at vasica8@seznam.cz. We will delete the usage statistics recorded with that code. Without the code we cannot find them, because they are not linked to your name or email address. Crash reports are deleted by themselves within 90 days. For purchase records, send us the Order ID from Apple's receipt email for Dailyvokab Pro (you can also find it at reportaproblem.apple.com), so we can find them in RevenueCat. For emails you sent us, write to the same address.

Your rights

We are based in the EU, so EU data protection law (the GDPR) gives you these rights about the data we hold about you, wherever you live:

  • to ask what data we hold about you, and to get a copy of it
  • to have it corrected
  • to have it deleted
  • to limit how it is used
  • to get it in a common file format, to take it somewhere else
  • to object to its use, for example by switching off “Help improve the app”
  • to complain to a data protection authority

Some of these rights apply only in some cases. To use one, write to vasica8@seznam.cz. It is free, and we answer within one month. Most data is linked only to random codes, so we may ask for your usage code to find yours.

You can complain to the data protection authority in the EU country where you live or work, or where you think the problem happened. In the Czech Republic, where we are based, this is the Office for Personal Data Protection (Úřad pro ochranu osobních údajů, ÚOOÚ): uoou.gov.cz.

We do not use your data to make automatic decisions about you. If we test two offers of Pro, which one you see is picked at random.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts collecting something new, this page will say so and the date at the top will change.
